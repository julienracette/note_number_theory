\chapter{Divisibility and Prime Numbers}
\intro{
    We start with the basic arithmetic of $\mathbb{Z}$: Euclidean division, the greatest common divisor and Bézout's identity. These lead to Euclid's lemma and to the fundamental theorem of arithmetic, which says that every integer $n \ge 2$ factors into primes in exactly one way.

    We end the chapter with Euclid's proof that there are infinitely many primes. The analytic part gives a quantitative version of this statement (see \cref{thm:Mertens}).
}
